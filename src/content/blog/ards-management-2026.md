<div class="keypoints">
<h2 id="key-points">Key points</h2>
<ul>
<li>The 2024 global definition lets you diagnose ARDS in patients on high-flow nasal oxygen at 30 L/min or more, using SpO2/FiO2 and lung ultrasound.<sup><a href="#r1">1</a></sup></li>
<li>High-flow nasal cannula is the first-line noninvasive support for de novo hypoxaemic respiratory failure in the 2026 ATS guideline.<sup><a href="#r3">3</a></sup></li>
<li>Low tidal volume and early, long prone sessions when PaO2/FiO2 is below 150 mmHg remain the strongest recommendations in ventilated ARDS.<sup><a href="#r4">4</a></sup></li>
<li>Corticosteroids now carry a conditional recommendation from both ATS and SCCM. The best drug, dose and duration remain unsettled.<sup><a href="#r6">6</a>,<a href="#r7">7</a></sup></li>
<li>Neuromuscular blockade and higher PEEP are where the societies diverge. Use them selectively, not by protocol default.</li>
<li>VV-ECMO belongs to selected severe ARDS after optimal conventional care, including a trial of prone positioning, in an experienced centre.<sup><a href="#r6">6</a>,<a href="#r9">9</a></sup></li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 id="definition">A broader definition and earlier recognition</h2>
<p>The 2012 Berlin definition required positive pressure ventilation with a PEEP of at least 5 cmH2O. Many patients with the same lung injury were managed on high-flow nasal oxygen and never met that criterion. The 2024 global definition keeps the Berlin framework and makes four changes:<sup><a href="#r1">1</a></sup></p>
<ol>
<li>Patients on high-flow nasal oxygen at 30 L/min or more can meet the definition.</li>
<li>Hypoxaemia can be defined by PaO2/FiO2 of 300 mmHg or less, or by SpO2/FiO2 of 315 or less when SpO2 is 97% or lower.</li>
<li>Bilateral opacities remain the imaging criterion, and lung ultrasound is accepted as an imaging method.</li>
<li>In resource-limited settings, PEEP, oxygen flow rate and a specific support device are not required.</li>
</ol>
<p>Early validation work suggests the change is not cosmetic. A 2026 study found that the global definition identified more patients than Berlin and made the diagnosis a median of three hours earlier.<sup><a href="#r2">2</a></sup></p>
<div class="callout"><strong>At the bedside</strong>The label of ARDS now applies before intubation. Lung-protective thinking, a conservative fluid plan and an escalation plan should start at that point, not after the tube.</div>

<h2 id="noninvasive">Before the tube: noninvasive support</h2>
<p>The 2026 ATS guideline on noninvasive respiratory support recommends high-flow nasal cannula over standard oxygen for adults with de novo acute hypoxaemic respiratory failure. It suggests noninvasive ventilation by facemask or helmet, and CPAP, as alternatives to standard oxygen.<sup><a href="#r3">3</a></sup></p>
<p>The 2023 ESICM guideline reached a similar position. It recommends high-flow oxygen over conventional oxygen to reduce intubation, with a strong recommendation and moderate certainty. It found no effect on mortality and made no recommendation on helmet versus facemask.<sup><a href="#r4">4</a></sup></p>
<p>The risk with any noninvasive strategy is delayed intubation in a patient who is failing. Neither guideline sets a time limit. Define failure criteria before you start, such as rising work of breathing, worsening oxygenation or new haemodynamic instability, and reassess within the first hours.</p>

<h2 id="ventilation">Ventilating the injured lung</h2>
<h3>Tidal volume</h3>
<p>ESICM recommends a tidal volume of 4 to 8 mL/kg of predicted body weight, as a strong recommendation, and extends it to COVID-19 ARDS.<sup><a href="#r4">4</a></sup> The 2017 ATS, ESICM and SCCM recommendations on low tidal volume and plateau pressure below 30 cmH2O remain in force.<sup><a href="#r5">5</a>,<a href="#r6">6</a></sup></p>
<h3>PEEP</h3>
<p>This is the clearest disagreement between societies.</p>

<p>In practice, a higher PEEP strategy is reasonable in moderate to severe ARDS, but check the response. Watch oxygenation, compliance, driving pressure and blood pressure after each change. A patient whose compliance falls and whose haemodynamics worsen at higher PEEP is telling you the lung did not recruit.</p>
<h3>Prone positioning</h3>
<p>Prone positioning carries the strongest evidence in this field. ESICM recommends it for moderate to severe ARDS, defined as PaO2/FiO2 below 150 mmHg on PEEP of at least 5 cmH2O after ventilator optimisation. It should start early after intubation, once tidal volume and PEEP are set, and each session should last at least 16 consecutive hours. Both statements are strong recommendations with high certainty.<sup><a href="#r4">4</a></sup></p>

<h2 id="adjuncts">Drug adjuncts: steroids and paralysis</h2>
<h3>Corticosteroids</h3>
<p>Both ATS and SCCM now suggest corticosteroids for adults with ARDS, as a conditional recommendation with moderate certainty.<sup><a href="#r6">6</a>,<a href="#r7">7</a></sup> The SCCM panel could not recommend a specific drug, dose or duration from the available evidence.<sup><a href="#r7">7</a></sup> Trial regimens differ. Choose one, apply it consistently in your unit, and weigh the risks of hyperglycaemia, infection and myopathy for each patient.</p>
<h3>Neuromuscular blockade</h3>
<p>The three societies read the same trials differently.</p>
<ul>
<li>ATS suggests neuromuscular blockers in early severe ARDS. Conditional, low certainty.<sup><a href="#r6">6</a></sup></li>
<li>ESICM recommends against routine continuous infusions to reduce mortality in moderate to severe ARDS. Strong, moderate certainty.<sup><a href="#r4">4</a></sup></li>
<li>The 2026 SCCM guideline makes a conditional recommendation to use neuromuscular blockers when PaO2/FiO2 is below 150.<sup><a href="#r8">8</a></sup></li>
</ul>
<p>These positions are less opposed than they look. None supports routine paralysis for every patient. All allow targeted use. In my practice, paralysis is reserved for early severe ARDS with persistent ventilator dyssynchrony, very high respiratory drive or refractory hypoxaemia despite deep sedation. It is given for a defined short period and reviewed daily.</p>

<h2 id="ecmo">Rescue therapy: VV-ECMO</h2>
<p>ATS suggests VV-ECMO in selected patients with severe ARDS, as a conditional recommendation with low certainty.<sup><a href="#r6">6</a></sup> ESICM based its position on a meta-analysis of the CESAR and EOLIA trials that showed lower 60-day mortality with ECMO, with a risk ratio of 0.72.<sup><a href="#r4">4</a></sup></p>
<p>The ELSO guideline sets the minimum threshold at refractory hypoxaemia with PaO2/FiO2 below 80 mmHg, or severe hypercapnia with pH below 7.25 and PaCO2 of 60 mmHg or more. Both apply only after optimal conventional management, including a trial of prone positioning unless contraindicated.<sup><a href="#r9">9</a></sup> ELSO also advises that if transfer will be needed, it should happen early.<sup><a href="#r9">9</a></sup></p>
<p>The choice between VV and VA support is covered in the companion article on <a href="ecmo-vv-vs-va-decision-framework.html">ECMO configuration</a>.</p>

<h2 id="sequence">A bedside sequence</h2>
<div class="callout"><strong>Author's synthesis</strong>This sequence combines the guidelines above with expert practice. It is a framework, not a protocol, and each step depends on the patient in front of you.</div>
<ol>
<li>Recognise ARDS early. Use SpO2/FiO2 and lung ultrasound if arterial gases or radiography are delayed.</li>
<li>Start high-flow nasal cannula for de novo hypoxaemic failure and write down the failure criteria.</li>
<li>After intubation, set tidal volume at 6 mL/kg predicted body weight, keep plateau pressure below 30 cmH2O and monitor driving pressure.</li>
<li>In moderate to severe ARDS, apply a higher PEEP strategy and keep it only if the lung responds.</li>
<li>If PaO2/FiO2 stays below 150 mmHg after optimisation, prone the patient early for at least 16 hours per session.</li>
<li>Consider corticosteroids if there is no contraindication.</li>
<li>Use neuromuscular blockade only for the targeted indications above, for a short period.</li>
<li>If PaO2/FiO2 stays below 80 mmHg or severe hypercapnic acidosis persists despite all of the above, call the ECMO centre early.</li>
</ol>

<h2 id="uncertain">What remains uncertain</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>PEEP individualisation.</strong> No method of setting PEEP has shown a mortality benefit over another.<sup><a href="#r4">4</a></sup></li>
<li><strong>Phenotypes.</strong> ESICM reviewed biological and clinical phenotypes but did not make graded recommendations, so phenotype-guided treatment remains a research tool.<sup><a href="#r4">4</a></sup></li>
<li><strong>Steroid regimen.</strong> The best drug, dose and duration have not been established.<sup><a href="#r7">7</a></sup></li>
<li><strong>Timing of escalation.</strong> How long to persist with noninvasive support before intubation remains a clinical judgement.</li>
</ul>
